What MishMash is, in five questions: why, what, who, where, when — and end with how you can take part. MishMash is one of the national AI research centres funded by the Research Council of Norway. We study AI from the perspective of creativity.

Why?

Because there is no agreement about what AI is doing to us — and that disagreement is the thing worth studying.

First question: why does a centre like this exist at all.

Why? · 2026

A mishmash of opinions

There is a confusing mixture — a mishmash — of notions about AI's potential and its risks.

Headlines from this year. Threat on one side: actors and musicians campaigning against training on their work, church musicians reacting to AI songs at funerals, UNESCO reporting falling income for artists. Promise on the other: transformed creativity, reinvented film production, productivity gains. Both are true at once, and that confusion is where the centre gets its name.

Why?

Both are true at once

Threat

  • Models trained on artists' work without agreement
  • Falling income across the creative sector
  • A narrower, more uniform culture
  • Energy, water and hardware

Promise

  • New instruments, new forms, new works
  • Archives that can finally be searched
  • Tools in the hands of people without a studio
  • Methods that travel out of the arts

Most public arguments pick a side. A research centre has to hold both.

You can find good evidence for either column. The mistake is to treat the question as settled in either direction. Our job is to produce evidence about specific systems in specific uses, rather than opinions about AI in general.

Why? · the name

mish·mash

noun — a confused mixture.

Not an acronym. It began as a working title, describing how the founders saw the group of people and institutions that had assembled.

Among more than 150 centre proposals pitched to the Research Council of Norway in 2024, it was one of the few names people remembered. So it stuck.

People always ask about the name. It is not an acronym. It describes the deliberate mix of artistic and scientific disciplines — and the fact that we are merging them into something new. The full story is on arj.no and in the FAQ on mishmash.no.

Why? · definitions

Is a machine creative?

Creativity — the ability to form novel and meaningful ideas or works. (Boden 2004)

Creative AI — machine systems that can produce novel and meaningful results that stand independently. (de Vries 2020)

Creativity is a fundamental human trait. It has shaped technology, and been shaped by it, for as long as there has been technology.

Two definitions we work from. Note that "novel and meaningful" is a demanding pair: a random generator is novel and not meaningful; a copy is meaningful and not novel. The open question is not whether a machine can be creative in the abstract, but how human and machine creativity differ, and how the two can be co-creative.

Why? · where we stand

All technology, AI included, is made by people and for people. The interesting things happen where the circles overlap.

Most attention goes to what the machines can do — benchmarks, capabilities, model releases. That is one circle. People get discussed as users, or as the ones being replaced. That is the other. MishMash lives in the overlap: the meeting point between human and machine, and between art and science.

Why? · our entry point

Why start with the arts

Artists have been putting these systems under pressure since the 1950s. They find the edges first.

They push harder

Artistic use breaks a system in ways benchmark tests never reach.

They ask first

Authorship, consent and ownership arrived in the arts before they arrived anywhere else.

They show the public

A performance or an exhibition puts the question in front of people who will never read a paper.

Artistic exploration is our entry point for critical discussion about AI and its implications for human–machine interaction and society.

This is the strategic bet of the centre. Not "AI applied to art", but art as the method for interrogating AI. If you work on AI anywhere else, the creative sector is a good place to look for the failure modes, and for the methods that keep people in control.

Second question: what the centre actually does.

What? · the primary objective

MishMash will
create, explore and reflect on
AI for, through and in
creative practices.

The whole centre in one sentence. Three verbs and three prepositions, and the prepositions matter. Create: build systems, tools, artworks, frameworks and policies. Explore: use AI in creative practice, and take creative methods into other domains. Reflect: study the impacts critically. AI for creative practices, AI through creative practices, AI in creative practices.

What? · three approaches

Create · explore · reflect

CREATE

Making AI-based systems, tools and artworks — and the frameworks and policies around them.

EXPLORE

Using AI in creative practice, and taking creative methods into other fields.

REFLECT

Critically studying what AI does to people, culture and society.

Every work package does all three. It is also not a bad description of what happens in any lab.

These three are also three kinds of people: engineers and artists create; educators, clinicians and curators explore; scholars from sociology, philosophy and law reflect. Usually each group works at its own level, alone. Putting them in the same room is most of what the centre is for.

What? · seven themes

Seven work packages

WP1AI for artistic performances — live music and art where people and machines improvise together
WP2AI in artistic processes — how AI changes the making of film, games, music and visual art
WP3Creative use of AI for health and well-being — arts therapies, emotion, consent
WP4Creative use of AI in education — AI literacy for schools and lifelong learning
WP5AI in the creative and cultural industries — copyright, business models, sustainability
WP6AI for cultural heritage — opening archives, libraries and museums
WP7Human-centric AI for creative problem-solving — from industrial design to emergency response
Seven themes, one work package each. Some use AI in artistic applications; others take creative methods into adjacent domains — health, education, heritage, problem-solving. Each WP has a lead and two sidekicks from different institutions, deliberately.

What? · the structure

Themes × approaches × perspectives. A structured mesh, built out of the mishmash.

The third axis is the perspectives: machines, humans, society. Every project sits somewhere in this cube. The point of the cube is that a project is never only a theme — it is a theme, an approach, and a perspective at once, which is what forces people to meet.

What? · three perspectives

Machines · humans · society

Machines

What can these systems actually do, and why? Data, models, limits.

Humans

How do we create with them? Agency, skill, bodies, craft.

Society

What changes when they enter the world? Authorship, law, economy, education.

Many disagreements about AI are really disagreements about which of these three you are talking about. A claim about what a model can do is not a claim about what it does to a profession.

What? · in practice

What it looks like

One picture per work package, from the people actually doing the work. Pick two or three to talk about depending on the room — for a general audience WP4 and WP6 usually land best.

What? · an example

Strings On-Line — self-playing guitars that sense the room. NIME 2020.

Acoustic guitars with microcomputers, sensors and actuators, so generated sound radiates from a wooden body rather than a loudspeaker. The input side is the point: the guitars sense people moving in the space, so the mapping from human action to sound is the design problem. Every system most people have met is disembodied — you type, a file comes back. A body changes what a system can know, and what it can be held to.

Who? · three kinds of people

Who creates, who explores, who reflects

Create

Engineers and artists. They build the systems and make the work.

Explore

Educators, clinicians, curators — the people who put the work in front of others.

Reflect

Scholars from sociology, philosophy and law. They ask what it means, and what changes.

Usually these three groups work at their own level, alone. Co-location is what changes that.

At RITMO we have had music researchers, psychologists and informatics people in the same building for nine years, sharing a coffee machine. That is what made the interdisciplinary work real rather than nominal. MishMash is distributed, so we have to manufacture that proximity — which is what the weekly and biweekly rhythm is for.

Who? · how disciplines meet

Intra, cross, multi, inter, trans. A centre needs all of them at different moments. Jensenius, Sound Actions (MIT Press, 2022)

Do you work interdisciplinary, or are you interdisciplinary? Most so-called interdisciplinary projects are cross-disciplinary: everyone stays comfortable in their own discipline and contributes a piece. The real thing starts when the terminology breaks down and people step on each other's toes. It is painful, and it is where the new things come from.

Who? · an example

Ten definitions of noise

At a MishMash workshop, ten people from different backgrounds each gave a ten-minute talk on one word: noise.

Randomness

the mathematician

A fluctuation

the physicist

The unwanted part

signal processing

Material

the sound artist

Disturbance

the philosopher

The signal

whoever is recording ventilation this summer

Ten definitions, no agreement, and a very good discussion. That is what multidisciplinary looks like in practice.

And it changes with what you are doing. I spent a summer recording ventilation systems for a project, so for me the ventilation was the signal, not the noise. The lesson: do not resolve the disagreement too early. The friction is the content.

How to take part

Online, Thursdays 12:00–12:30. A talk and a discussion. Open to everyone, no registration.

Sign up for one or more work packages. Free. Open to researchers at partner institutions and to the unaffiliated. Write to contact@mishmash.no.

Two a year, touring Norway. Talks, performances and exhibitions in one programme.

Apply as a master fellow

Master's students at affiliated institutions can apply to become a MishMash Master Fellow.

Membership costs nothing and commits you to nothing except showing up. Members can apply for seed funding together with other partners. For a public audience, the MeshUp is the honest recommendation: turn up once, see whether it is for you.

Join · an experiment you can inspect

The website is part of the research

  • Person profiles generated from the national research archive (NVA), or ORCID for those outside Norway.
  • Three reading levels on key pages — short, standard, advanced. Ted Nelson's stretchtext, finally buildable.
  • Automatic Norwegian translation, which every communications office would be sceptical about. That is why we do it.
  • At the bottom of every page: who edited it, the licence, the privacy level, the accessibility level.
  • The whole site is public on GitHub.

Every file

Every file should carry an explicit licence and an explicit privacy level — not the project, not the folder, the file. Do that and training data, sharing and reproducibility all become tractable, because you can follow what happened to what.

mishmash.no/about/ai-colophon/

The site is itself a creative use of AI, and it is open about it. We want to try things, test things, break things — and say so when they break. The "every file" point is the practical appeal to anyone who handles data.
